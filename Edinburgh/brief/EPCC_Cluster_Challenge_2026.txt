CIUK Cluster Challenge 2026 – EPCC Challenge
Welcome to the EPCC Challenge. By now, you should have completed the Driving
Test, received your Cirrus access, and become familiar with the system. We expect
you’ll be referring frequently to the Cirrus documentation. Every team has a capped
allocation, so save your budget by running short test jobs during setup and tuning.
To avoid surprises, request dedicated node access using #SBATCH --exclusive in
your job scripts.

For this challenge, you will run, analyse, and optimise two important benchmarks:
HPL (High Performance Linpack) and HPCG (High Performance Conjugate
Gradient). These benchmarks are widely used in the TOP500 and Green500
rankings to evaluate the performance and energy efficiency of supercomputers. Your
goal is to balance high performance with energy efficiency. You can scale your
experiments up to 4 nodes on Cirrus. You will submit your results, binaries, scripts,
and a short presentation describing your findings.


High Performance Linpack (HPL)
HPL is a compute intensive benchmark that measures how many floating-point
operations per second (FLOPS) a system can perform. It stresses the CPU and
floating-point performance, making it a classic benchmark for peak computational
efficiency.

   •   Compile and run HPL on Cirrus.
   •   Scale your runs from 1 node up to a maximum of 4 nodes.
   •   Measure:
          o Runtime (time-to-solution).
          o Performance (GFLOPS).
          o Energy consumption (using the sacct command).
          o Energy efficiency (GFLOPS/W).
   •   Analyse the trade-offs between adding more nodes and energy-to-solution.
   •   Optimise your runs by exploring:
          o Problem size selection.
          o Compiler optimisation flags.
          o MPI/OpenMP configuration.


Deliverables
   •   Compiled binaries of HPL used in your experiments.
   •   Run scripts and input files so results can be reproduced.
   •   Results summary (tables/plots of runtime, GFLOPS, Joules, GFLOPS/W).
   •   Presentation slides (5–7 min) explaining:
          o Your optimisation strategies.
          o Best results achieved.
          o Lessons learned about performance vs energy efficiency.
High Performance Conjugate Gradient (HPCG)
HPCG is a benchmark designed to better reflect real-world scientific workloads,
which are often memory-bound and communication-heavy. HPCG stresses memory
bandwidth, network latency, and data movement. It is increasingly seen as a
complement to HPL in benchmarking supercomputers.

   •   Compile and run HPCG on Cirrus.
   •   Scale your runs from 1 node up to 4 nodes.
   •   Submitted jobs must have run for at least 30 mins.
   •   Use short tests (e.g. 1 min) for tuning to save core hours.
   •   All final logs must pass validation (Result is VALID).

   •   Measure:
         o Runtime.
         o Performance (GFLOPS).
         o Energy consumption.
         o Energy efficiency (GFLOPS/W).

   •   Explore optimisations such as:
          o Problem size configuration.
          o MPI/OpenMP hybridisation.
          o Compiler flags.



Deliverables
   •   Compiled binaries of HPCG used in your experiments.
   •   Run scripts and input files so results can be reproduced.
   •   Results summary (tables/plots of runtime, GFLOPS, Joules, GFLOPS/W).
   •   Presentation slides (5–7 min) explaining:
          o Your optimisation strategies.
          o Best results achieved.
          o Insights on differences between compute-bound (HPL) and memory-
             bound (HPCG) workloads.
